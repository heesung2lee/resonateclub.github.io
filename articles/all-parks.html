California's Nature & Parks

California packs nine national parks — more than any other state — plus a chain of deserts, alpine lakes and offshore islands. This hub links ten full nature guides, from granite valleys to salt flats to 300-foot redwoods. Use it to match a park to the season: deserts shine October–April, the Sierra opens June–September, and the redwood coast stays green year-round. Entrance fees run $30–$35 per vehicle at the big parks (good for 7 days), and an $80 annual pass pays off on a third visit. Summer weekends demand reservations at Yosemite and timed entries at Muir Woods; winter means chain controls over mountain passes. Pick the landscape first, then let the linked guide handle trails, drives and lodging.

Guides in this section

  • Yosemite

    Granite walls rise 3,000 feet above a river valley, with waterfalls peaking in late May and the Mist Trail's 1.6-mile climb to Vernal Fall footbridge as the classic first hike. The $35 vehicle fee covers 7 days, and peak-season day-use reservations book out weeks ahead. Campgrounds release 5 months out at 7am Pacific. Tip: enter before 8am or ride the free valley shuttle to skip midday parking jams.

  • Redwood National & State Parks

    Coast redwoods top 370 feet, the tallest living things on Earth, in groves 5–6 hours north of San Francisco. Tall Trees Grove needs a free permit; Fern Canyon's 50-foot walls require a short wade. Summer fog keeps the forest damp and cool while inland valleys bake. Tip: pair the Newton B. Drury Scenic Parkway with Gold Bluffs Beach at low tide.

  • Sequoia & Kings Canyon

    General Sherman stands 275 feet tall with a 36-foot trunk diameter — the largest tree on Earth by volume — beside 14,000-foot Sierra peaks. The Generals Highway climbs 5,000 feet in 30 twisting miles, so allow an hour each way. Snow lingers into May; chains are required November–April. Tip: descend into Kings Canyon's floor for waterfalls without the Valley crowds.

  • Death Valley

    Badwater Basin sits 282 feet below sea level, the lowest point in North America, under summer highs past 120°F (49°C). Visit October–April, when daytime temperatures run 65–85°F and night skies earn dark-sky status. Furnace Creek lodging books out for spring wildflower season. Tip: carry a gallon of water per person and fuel up — stations inside the park charge steep premiums.

  • Joshua Tree

    Two deserts meet among monzogranite boulders and spiky namesake trees, 45 minutes from Palm Springs. The 4.5-mile Ryan Mountain trail gives the best sunrise view; Keys View overlooks the Coachella Valley from 5,185 feet. Campgrounds fill October–April. Tip: enter by 9am on weekends or use the quieter south entrance near Cottonwood.

  • Channel Islands

    Five roadless islands 1–2 hours by ferry from Ventura, home to endemic island foxes found nowhere else. Day trips run $60–$100 round trip; Santa Cruz Island's Potato Harbor hike covers 5 miles of sea cliffs. No services exist ashore, so pack food and water. Tip: spring crossings bring gray whales and wildflower hillsides in one trip.

  • Lake Tahoe

    An alpine lake 6,225 feet up with water clear past 60 feet deep, ringed by 300-plus inches of annual snowfall at the resorts. Summer brings the 165-mile Tahoe Rim Trail and $20–$40 kayak rentals; winter lift tickets run $150–$250. The 72-mile lakeside drive takes 3 hours without stops. Tip: stay on the north shore for quiet, the south shore for nightlife.

  • Mammoth Lakes

    A ski mountain with 3,500 skiable acres and an 11,053-foot summit pairs with summer access to Devils Postpile's basalt columns, 20 minutes down the road. Lift tickets match Tahoe pricing; the town shuttle runs free in winter. Hot Creek's geothermal pools sit 15 minutes east. Tip: buy lift tickets online 7+ days ahead for the steepest discounts.

  • Mendocino County

    Redwood canyons and sea cliffs 3.5 hours north of San Francisco, with the 100-year-old Skunk Train running 40-mile redwood routes near $50–$80. Glass Beach in Fort Bragg glitters with decades-old sea glass, and the coast botanical gardens bloom April–June. Tip: Highway 1 narrows to one lane in stretches — drive it in daylight and fuel up in Bodega Bay.

  • Santa Cruz

    Ninety minutes south of San Francisco, Santa Cruz mixes a 1907 seaside boardwalk (ride wristbands near $40–$60) with redwood groves 20 minutes inland at Henry Cowell. Steamer Lane draws year-round surfers; fall brings monarch butterflies to Natural Bridges. Tip: park once downtown and walk — the beach, wharf and main street sit within a mile.

Park Passes & Seasonal Strategy

Stack visits to make passes pay. The $80 America the Beautiful pass covers all federal parks for a year, beating three $30–$35 single-park fees on any Yosemite–Death Valley–Joshua Tree loop. Reserve summer lodging 4–6 months ahead near Yosemite and Tahoe; desert inns fill for February–March wildflower season instead. Shoulder months (April–May, September–October) deliver open Sierra roads, manageable desert heat and thinner crowds almost everywhere. Always check road status before a mountain trip — Tioga Pass typically opens late May and closes in November, and Highway 1 slides close the Big Sur stretch with little notice.

Start here: Yosemite and the redwoods are the icons; the desert parks and islands are the hidden treasures.
