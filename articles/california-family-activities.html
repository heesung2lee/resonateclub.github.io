California Family Activities Guide: Theme Parks, Beaches & More

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

California is one of the great family playgrounds in the United States, and the variety is remarkable. In a single trip you can ride theme-park roller coasters in the morning, dip your toes in the Pacific by the afternoon and stand under a giant redwood the next day. The state is compact enough to combine a city, a beach and a national park in one vacation, yet varied enough to keep every age group occupied. With a little planning, a family vacation here goes a long way — here is how to lock in the highlights without burning out the kids.

Theme Parks

The state’s biggest draw for families is its theme parks. Disneyland Resort in Anaheim is celebrating its 70th anniversary, making it an especially good time to visit. Universal Studios Hollywood offers movie-based rides and the Studio Tour, while Legoland California in Carlsbad is built for younger kids with hands-on building areas and gentle rides. Knott’s Berry Farm in Buena Park pairs theme-park thrills with Old West history. Buy tickets online in advance; on many days the rides themselves are only part of the fun.

Beaches

California’s beaches are famously family-friendly. La Jolla Shores has gentle water and soft sand, ideal for toddlers. Coronado offers the wide, calm beach in front of the historic Hotel del Coronado. Santa Monica brings a pier with rides and an aquarium, and Huntington Beach delivers surf culture and miles of sand. Little ones tend to do best in the cooler morning hours, so hit the sand early and pack plenty of water and sunscreen.

Zoos & Aquariums

World-class animal attractions are easy to find. The legendary San Diego Zoo is a full day, with mountains and a breathtaking range of species. Coastal favorites include the Monterey Bay Aquarium, a pioneering marine research center, and Aquarium of the Bay in San Francisco with a walk-through tunnel. For a more compact stop, Birch Aquarium overlooks the sea in La Jolla.

Nature & Outdoors

Beyond the crowds, California’s landscapes are a lesson waiting to happen. Yosemite gives kids grand granite cliffs and waterfalls, while the northern redwood forests shelter the tallest trees on Earth. Tide pools are a hit for snorkeling and beachcombing, and winter whale watching cruises from the coast bring migrating gray whales into view. It is hard to beat the look on a child’s face at the first whale sighting.

Hands-On Museums

When the weather is less than perfect, museums deliver. The California Science Center in Los Angeles is home to the space shuttle Endeavour, and Exploratorium in San Francisco is an entire building of hands-on exhibits that kids never want to leave. Families with younger explorers will enjoy the Bay Area Discovery Museum, and the Tech Interactive in San Jose focuses on technology and innovation.

Tips for Traveling with Kids

Think in multi-day itineraries rather than rushing through a checklist. Book theme parks and top attractions in advance to skip lines and save money, and target weekdays, when parks and museums are noticeably less crowded. Combo passes and multi-park tickets can shave real money off a family budget, and many museums are free on specific days or in the late afternoon. Keep water and snacks within reach, build in breaks, and remember that hands-on exhibits and downtime are as important as the headline ride.

When to Go

Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons almost everywhere, with mild weather and thinner crowds at theme parks. Summer is prime time for the beach but brings heat inland and long lines at the biggest parks, so plan weekday visits and arrive before opening. Winter is the quietest and most affordable, though coastal weather is cool and several mountain destinations are at their most dramatic. Whatever the month, check opening hours and book the headline attractions early.

Pace yourself: California is big. A family trip that tries to hit Disneyland, the coast, Yosemite and the redwoods all in one week will feel frantic. Pick two or three regions and give each a couple of relaxed days.
