PGA West Stadium Course

Updated August 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

If you've ever heard a golfer describe a course they both love and fear, they were probably talking about the PGA West TPC Stadium Course. Built by Pete and Alice Dye and opened in 1986, the Stadium is widely considered the toughest and most famous of the courses at the PGA West complex in La Quinta — and it's the one that put desert golf's reputation for drama on the map.

The desert monster

The Stadium course is not gentle. It wraps around desert scrub, water hazards and Dye's trademark harsh, deep bunkers. Water comes into play on fully half the holes, most notoriously around the turn, on the closing stretch, and at the island-green par-3 17th. When it debuted, it was so demanding that touring pros reportedly objected to the difficulty — which should tell you everything about what to expect.

The "Alcatraz" 17th

The cavernous par-3 17th is the round's defining moment. It's a short-to-mid iron over water to a tiny, heavily bunkered island green. Wind off the nearby mountains makes club selection a gamble every time. A ball in the water — or in the surrounding bunkers — is a very real outcome; holding the green is a genuine achievement. "Alcatraz," as it's nicknamed, is one of the most fearsome par 3s anywhere, and it's the hole you'll talk about long after the round.

Why it's more popular than the Mountain

PGA West actually has several layouts, including the Pete Dye Mountain course and the Nicklaus Tournament course. But the Stadium is the one that draws the crowds. It's the bucket-list round — the course players have read about, seen on TV (it hosted the PGA Tour's "Q-School" finals for years and the famous Skins Game) and want to test themselves against. If you only play one round in the Coachella Valley, this is the one.

What it costs & how to book

Green fees at the Stadium course typically run around $150–$250, though the exact price varies by season and time of day. It's a public course, so any golfer can book — but because it's the most in-demand layout in the desert, weekday and twilight tee times are far easier to secure than peak weekends. Book ahead, especially in the cooler season (October–April).

Traveler's advice: Treat the Stadium as an adventure, not a scorecard round. Bring plenty of balls, enjoy the drama of the island green, and if it's your first desert golf experience, pair it with the friendlier Mountain or Nicklaus courses for a more relaxed day.

Planning the trip

The Stadium course sits in La Quinta, about 20 minutes from Palm Springs and roughly two hours east of Los Angeles. The Coachella Valley's golf season runs October through April, when conditions are ideal. You can easily combine the Stadium with a look at other Pacific Coast or desert courses on the same trip — see our Palm Springs & Desert Golf and California Golf Road Trip guides for ideas.
