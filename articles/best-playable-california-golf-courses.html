Best Playable Golf Courses in California

Updated August 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

California has more than a hundred course ranked among America's best — but a surprising number of them are private clubs you cannot get into without an invitation. So we did the practical thing: we looked at the state's most-loved courses and picked the ones a traveling golfer can actually book. No member-only asides, no "if only you knew a member" teasers. These are the courses worth your money and your tee time, with a realistic sense each one of what they cost and how they feel.

Why "playable" matters

The first thing to understand about California golf is the split between gorgeous private clubs and the public courses open to everyone. Iconic names like Cypress Point and Los Angeles Country Club are routinely ranked at the top — but they're invitation-only. The courses below are the ones that put world-class golf within reach of a visitor, whether you're splurging on a bucket-list round or looking for a great-value day.

The bucket-list resorts

These are the famous ones — worth the price of admission and the advance planning.

  • Pebble Beach Golf Links (Monterey, from ~$500+) — the single most iconic public round in America. Cliff-hugging holes over Stillwater Cove, a storied host of the AT&T Pro-Am, and the closest thing to a golf pilgrimage on the West Coast. Book months ahead and budget accordingly.
  • Spyglass Hill (Pebble Beach Resort, ~$440) — the "hardest" of the resort's trio. Long, tree-lined and relentlessly demanding, it's a different beast from Pebble and arguably the better golf test.
  • The Links at Spanish Bay (Pebble Beach Resort, ~$340) — the most affordable piece of the trilogy. A genuine links-style round, windy and exposed, famous for the bagpiper who plays at sunset.
  • Torrey Pines South (San Diego, ~$270) — a public municipal that hosts the PGA Tour's Farmers Insurance Open every January. Ocean bluffs on nearly every hole, and a layout that holds up against the pros.

Desert drama

The Palm Springs desert is a golf destination in its own right, and much of it is thankfully open to play.

  • PGA West (TPC Stadium) (La Quinta, ~$200) — Pete Dye's infamous "Alcatraz" island-green 17th. Genuinely one of the toughest and most talk-about resort courses anywhere.
  • PGA West (Nicklaus Tournament / Dye Mountain) (La Quinta, ~$150) — the friendlier siblings in the same complex; more forgiving, still thrilling, great value.
  • Indian Wells Resort (Players) (Indian Wells, ~$140) — a beautifully manicured desert resort that's scenic and fair.
  • Rams Hill (Borrego Springs, ~$130) — a Tom Fazio desert escape east of San Diego. Big sky, big view, and often surprisingly quiet.

Great-value hidden gems

Not every great California round costs hundreds of dollars. These are the ones locals quietly love.

  • Rustic Canyon (Moorpark / LA, ~$90) — a walkable, linksy municipal considered one of the best public courses in the country. Understated, fun and a genuine bargain.
  • Pacific Grove Golf Links (Monterey, ~$50) — the "poor man's Pebble Beach." A charming public track right on the bay that punches far above its price.
  • Barona Creek (San Diego, ~$120) — tribal-owned and immaculately kept, with real value and rarely crowded.
  • Half Moon Bay (Ocean) (Bay Area, ~$180) — cliff-top links with a dramatic finishing hole below the Ritz-Carlton; a favorite for a scenic coastal round.
  • Sandpiper (Santa Barbara, ~$110) — a linksy public course with sweeping Pacific views along the coast.
  • CordeValle (Santa Cruz area, ~$250) — a hidden Robert Trent Jones Jr. resort course tucked in the hills, close to Silicon Valley.
Traveler's advice: If you want the iconic experience, Pebble Beach is a bucket-list splurge worth saving for. If you want a beautiful, memorable day without the five-figure bill, Torrey Pines South, the warm desert courses, or underdog gems like Rustic Canyon and Pacific Grove deliver enormous bang for the buck.

How to plan a golf trip

California is a big state, so don't try to hit everything in one trip. Pick a region — Monterey, the desert, San Diego or the Bay Area — and play two or three courses near each other to cut drive time. The Monterey Peninsula alone offers Pebble, Spyglass, Spanish Bay and the affordable Pacific Grove all within minutes of each other, making it the single best golf concentration in the state.

Book well ahead for the famous resorts (Pebble, Torrey Pines) and stay flexible for the rest. Weekday, twilight and off-season slots are far easier to secure and often significantly cheaper.
