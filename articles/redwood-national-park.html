Redwood National & State Parks Guide: Trees, Drives & Best Stops

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

In the damp, foggy forests of far Northern California stand the tallest living things on Earth — the coast redwoods. Redwood National and State Parks is a joint federal-state network of four parks that preserves these ancient giants along the state’s north coast. It’s a cool, hushed and awe-inspiring destination.

About the Park Cluster

The complex includes Redwood National Park plus Del Norte Coast, Jedediah Smith and Prairie Creek Redwoods State Parks. Together they protect roughly 139,000 acres of redwood forest, oak woodlands, rivers and 37 miles of Pacific coastline.

  • Jedediah Smith Redwoods — in the north, driest and home to lush riverside groves.
  • Prairie Creek Redwoods — famous for fern-rich canyons and Roosevelt elk.
  • Del Norte Coast — dense temperate rainforest hugging the coast.
  • Redwood National Park — the federally managed heart of the network.

Drives & Scenic Byways

The best way to experience the redwoods is by car, with frequent stops.

Howland Hill Road (Jedediah Smith)

A winding narrow dirt-and-pavement road through old-growth redwoods, past the famous Stout Grove. Slow and magical.

Newton B. Drury Scenic Parkway (Prairie Creek)

A paved valley road beneath towering redwoods. Roosevelt elk often graze the meadows beside it.

Coastal Drive

A scenic cliff route along the Pacific with sweeping ocean views and gravel sections.

Must-See Sights

  • Stout Grove — a celebrated grove on a mossy river flat in Jedediah Smith.
  • Fern Canyon — a gorge walled in ferns; you may recognize it from film sets.
  • Trillium Falls Trail — a short loop through old growth in Prairie Creek.
  • Tall Tree Grove and Boy Scout Tree Trail — among the largest accessible specimens.
  • Gold Bluffs Beach — a remote beach edged by redwood bluffs.

Hiking the Redwoods

Trails range from paved family walks to strenuous backcountry. Highlights include the Big Tree Wayside, Lady Bird Johnson Grove and the long James Irvine Trail. Many trails are short and flat, making the park accessible to all ages.

Check closures & permits: Some areas, including Fern Canyon, require timed reservations during peak summer months, and the park occasionally has section closures. Verify conditions on the park’s official site before your trip.

Best Time to Visit

The north coast is often cool, misty and rainy. Summer (June–September) is the driest and most popular, with fewer showers and longer days — but you’ll share the trails. Spring and fall offer manageable weather and smaller crowds. Winter brings heavy rain, some storms and the lushest green — beautiful, but with closures and muddy trails.

Practical Tips

  1. Fuel up before entering. Services are limited between the small coastal towns.
  2. Dress in layers. It can be cool and damp even in summer; bring a rain shell.
  3. Stay in a nearby base town. Crescent City, Klamath and Orick are convenient.
  4. Respect the trees. Stay on trails to protect fragile root systems and mossy undergrowth.
  5. Watch for wildlife. Elk share the meadows and roads — drive slowly.
