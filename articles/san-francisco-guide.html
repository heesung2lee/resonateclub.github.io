San Francisco Travel Guide: Fisherman’s Wharf, Alcatraz & the Golden Gate

Updated AUGUST 2026 · GoldenPath Guide
· By GoldenPath Guide Editorial Team

San Francisco packs an outsized amount of charm into fewer than fifty square miles. It is a city of steep streets, fog, ferries and cable cars, where you can eat seafood on a working wharf in the morning and stand on a hilltop overlooking the whole bay by afternoon. Much of the best stuff is walkable, and the public transit is good enough that you can happily leave the car behind.

Fisherman’s Wharf & the Waterfront

The Wharf is the city’s most famous corner, and it is still a working waterfront at heart, with fishing boats unloading and crab pots stacked along the piers. The signature meal is clam chowder served in a sourdough bread bowl. For a classic sit-down version, park yourself at Scoma’s or Cioppino’s, both long-standing favorites that turn out fresh seafood with bay views. Then wander the piers, watch the boats, and take in the sounds of sea lions barking on the nearby docks.

Alcatraz Island

Just over a mile off the shore sits Alcatraz, the former maximum-security prison on a 22-acre island that once held Al Capone and Machine Gun Kelly. The ferry ride itself is scenic, and the audio tour that walks you through the cellhouse is widely considered one of the best in the country. Because capacity is limited, book tickets well in advance — daytime, nighttime and behind-the-scenes tours all sell out, and the evening cruises offer a particularly atmospheric look at the island after dark. Bring a jacket; it is surprisingly cold and breezy out on the bay.

Pier 39 & the Sea Lions

At the heart of the Wharf district, Pier 39 is home to the famous sea lions that haul themselves onto the K-Dock, where hundreds of the creatures gather and bark through the day. In 2024 the colony peaked at around 2,100 animals — each one capable of reaching 800 pounds. It is the closest most visitors get to California’s marine life up close, and a delight for kids and first-timers alike. The same complex is also home to the Aquarium of the Bay, a great indoor option if the fog rolls in.

Lombard Street & the Painted Ladies

Lombard Street’s steep, switchback block on Russian Hill has earned the nickname the “crookedest street in the world,” and the Hyde–Leavenworth stretch is the famous one. It is a fun photograph and a slow, winding descent. Across town, the Painted Ladies at Alamo Square are a row of painted Victorian houses that frame the city skyline behind them — the classic postcard shot of San Francisco, best at golden hour.

Golden Gate Bridge, Park & Chinatown

The Golden Gate Bridge needs no introduction. Walk or bike across it for the best views, or cross via ferry and ride back with the skyline behind you. Golden Gate Park spans more than a thousand acres of gardens, trails and museums — a welcome pocket of green. Chinatown, one of the oldest and largest in North America, is dense, lively and full of good food. The Embarcadero’s ferries make for an easy, scenic way to reach the East Bay.

Neighborhoods & the Embarcadero

Between the big sights, the city’s neighborhoods make it worth wandering. The Mission District is famous for its murals and taquerias, North Beach recalls the city’s Italian roots, and the waterfront Embarcadero is a broad, flat promenade ideal for walking or cycling. Each corner has its own pace, and dipping in and out gives you a feel for the city beyond the postcard landmarks.

Best Time to Visit

Spring and fall are the sweet spots, with mild temperatures and fewer crowds. Summer is peak season, though the famous coastal fog often rolls in through the morning before burning off. Pack layers, because the temperature can swing from sun to chill in a single afternoon — and a good jacket is worth bringing almost any month.

Getting around: You do not need a car. Ride the historic cable cars, take Muni buses and the BART system, and walk the central neighborhoods. Parking is expensive and the hills are steep, so public transit and your own two feet are the way to go.
