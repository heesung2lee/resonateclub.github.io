California Art Museums: From SFMOMA to the Getty & The Broad

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

California collects art the way it does almost everything else — on a grand and eclectic scale. From the Western’s largest modern-art collection to museums housed in some of the most striking architecture ever built, the state’s cultural institutions are destination-worthy in their own right. Many are free or offer free days, and the buildings are often as compelling as the canvases inside.

San Francisco & the Bay Area

The Bay Area also rewards the casual, drop-in visitor: several institutions land within a compact, walkable stretch of downtown, so it is easy to see two or three in a single day. Rainy seasons are a fine time to duck inside, while summer sun makes it pleasant to linger in the plazas and sculpture gardens that frame these buildings.

In downtown San Francisco, SFMOMA is the largest modern and contemporary art museum in the American West, a vast, light-filled complex (its facade by Snøhetta is memorable) with deep holdings that span photography, painting and sculpture. Across town in Golden Gate Park, the de Young Museum presents fine art and American painting inside a copper-clad building that overlooks the park. The same park is home to the California Academy of Sciences, so you can easily pair a visit.

The Getty & the LA Museums

Los Angeles may be the single richest one-stop city for art. The Getty Center, perched on a hilltop above the city, pairs world-class collections with gardens designed by Robert Irwin and panoramic views. Down the coast, the Getty Villa recreates a Roman villa to house its antiquities. Legendary architect Richard Meier designed the Center, making the architecture itself a highlight.

  • Getty Center — fine art, gardens and sweeping views; free with a timed reservation.
  • LACMA — a huge encyclopedic collection, fronted by the famous Urban Light sculpture of 202 vintage streetlamps.
  • The Broad — a contemporary collection in a porous honeycomb building, across the street from Disney Concert Hall.
  • MOCA — dedicated to contemporary art across a few LA locations.

The Crocker & Beyond

Statewide, don’t overlook the Crocker Art Museum in Sacramento — the oldest art museum on the West Coast, with a collection that ranges from European painting to California art in a landmark Victorian mansion setting. It is a rewarding stop for anyone passing through the capital. From here, smaller regional galleries throughout the state round out the picture.

Making the Most of the Visit

Planning is what turns a museum stop from a quick look into a rewarding afternoon.

Weekends can be busy, so a weekday visit or a first-thing morning slot tends to be quieter. Museum cafés and shops are worth a look too, and most big institutions are set up for families, with kid-friendly trails and audio guides.

  1. Check free days. Many major museums offer free or discounted admission on certain days or evenings.
  2. Book timed tickets. The Getty, The Broad and SFMOMA often require advance timed entry.
  3. Appreciate the architecture. Groups the Getty, Disney Hall and SFMOMA as much for their buildings as their art.
  4. Pace yourself. A curated few hours beat rushing a whole collection.
Getting around: Los Angeles’ museums are spread out, so allow drive time between sites. In San Francisco and Sacramento you can walk or use transit between venues.
