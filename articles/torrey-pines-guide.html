Torrey Pines: South & North

Updated August 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

Set on the bluffs of La Jolla, just north of San Diego, Torrey Pines is the most famous municipal golf facility in America — and arguably in the world. Two championship courses, a pair of U.S. Opens, and some of the most dramatic Pacific Ocean views in the sport make it a bucket-list destination for any golfer. That it is publicly owned and open to reservation is what makes it even more remarkable.

The South course

The South is the star. Remodeled by Rees Jones for the 2008 U.S. Open and tweaked again for 2021, it ranks among the longest and most testing courses on the PGA Tour at roughly 7,800 yards from the tips. It heads out along the cliffs, turns into the pine-studded interior, and comes home with a gripping run of finishing holes framed by the ocean.

The most famous stretch is the par-3 third, set above the gorge, followed by a par-5 that edges along the rim of the cliffs. The par-3 sixth is equally celebrated — a mid-iron over a cove to a green perched before the sea. Together they form one of the great back-nine starts in championship golf.

Famous moments

Much of the course’s legend comes from the tournaments it has hosted. The Farmers Insurance Open has been a fixture here since the 1950s, but it was the 2008 U.S. Open that made Torrey Pines immortal, when Tiger Woods won on a famously injured leg on the 72nd hole and then in an 18-hole playoff the following day. The 2021 U.S. Open returned, and the course now hosts both the Farmers Insurance Open and, on occasion, the game’s biggest events.

The North course

The North is the more forgiving sibling. Tom Weiskopf’s 2016 renovation softened the bunkering, widened many fairways and improved walkability, all while keeping the skyline views that define the property. For golfers who want the Torrey Pines experience without the South’s brutal length, the North is the right choice — and it hosts the second days of the Farmers Insurance Open, giving you the chance to watch the professionals play a course you can actually tackle.

Booking and tee times

Torrey Pines is a public, city-owned facility, which means anyone can play — but that also means demand is enormous. Tee times open on a rolling window, sell out quickly for weekends, and the South carries a premium rate on top of the standard greens fee.

  • Residents get a break. San Diego city residents pay far less and get access to a booking window that opens first. Visitors pay the full rate.
  • Book as early as you can. For peak weekends, reservations often go within minutes of opening. Plan the moment the window opens if a specific date matters.
  • The twilight window. Late-afternoon tee times (often called twilight) are cheaper and can be glorious — but you may not finish all 18 before dark, so budget your pace.
  • Be flexible on the course. If the South is sold out or too long, the North often has availability and is well worth a round.

Plan around the weather

San Diego is mild year-round, and the course sits on a coastal bluff, so temperatures rarely reach the inland highs. Expect a coastal breeze — it can be a full club or two at the exposed holes. Fog sometimes rolls in during the cooler months, burning off by mid-morning, so a slightly later tee time can mean clearer skies.

Course tip: Read the wind and pick your tees wisely. At 7,800-plus yards from the tips, the South is a brutal test for anyone but a scratch player. Most visitors find the middle tees provide the day’s reward without turning it into an endurance trial.

Before you go

  1. Research the current fee structure and resident-vs-visitor rates on the official municipal site.
  2. Decide South or North — or make it a two-day trip and play both, which many visitors do.
  3. Book a tee time as far ahead as the booking window allows.
  4. Plan around the Farmers Insurance Open dates if you’d like to watch the pros.
  5. Leave time to walk the bluffs above the ocean, which are free and open to the public and offer some of San Diego’s best views.

Whether you are chasing the course Tiger conquered or simply want one of golf’s great views, Torrey Pines is pure California golf at its most spectacular.
