California Movie Locations Tour: Studios, Iconic Spots & the Mother Road

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

No state has done more to define how the world imagines cinema than California. From the soundstages of Hollywood to the otherworldly crags where alien worlds were shot, a film-location road trip lets you stand where your favorite scenes were made — and it is a wonderfully varied way to see the Golden State. Here is how to plan one. The drive from a soundstage to a desert crag is a movie in itself — restless light, changing scenery, the outside world in fast-forward.

Hollywood & the Studio Lots

Start where the magic happens. Universal Studios Hollywood pairs its famous studio tour with a working backlot and theme-park rides, offering a glimpse behind the curtain of real productions. Paramount Pictures operates a historic lot tour through its soundstages and streets. 20th Century Studios sits among the other storied lots, and a mix of public tours and on-location trailers means there is always something recognizable on screen in a new release. Arriving early and booking ahead is essential, since access is limited and changes often.

Downtown LA & the Classic Landmarks

Downtown Los Angeles is packed with recognizable backdrops. The Bradbury Building, with its wrought-iron atrium and glass skylight, is a star in its own right — most famous today as a location in Blade Runner and countless other films. Just up the street, the little funicular railway Angels Flight is a period piece in miniature. For sweeping views of the city with cinematic pedigree, head to the Griffith Observatory, whose terrace is an icon from Rebel Without a Cause and La La Land. From the terrace the city unrolls toward the ocean, glittering, the air thin and clear. Intertwined through it all, the Judge Harry Pregerson Interchange and the downtown freeways have been shot on film more times than anyone can count.

The Desert & Country Backdrops

Head outside the city and California’s landscapes become movie sets themselves. Vasquez Rocks, spiky sandstone formations north of LA, have doubled for alien planets in Star Trek and countless other productions since the dawn of film. Further north near Lone Pine, the Alabama Hills — rounded boulders beneath the towering Sierra Nevada — were the backdrop for generations of westerns. And the iconic Route 66, the “Mother Road,” drifts through deserts, roadside diners and open horizon, a film-ready tableau its entire length. For a dose of classic Hollywood wonder, Disneyland rounds out the themed experience.

Planning Your Tour

A film-location road trip takes a little coordination.

The best part of a film-location tour is that the landmarks are also genuinely great places to visit. The Griffith Observatory offers one of the finest views of downtown LA, the Alabama Hills sit beside some of the most dramatic Sierra scenery in the state, and Vasquez Rocks is a fun trail to walk. In other words, the movie angle is the hook — but the landscape does the rest of the work. Standing in the ruins of a western set beneath the Sierras, it’s the quiet that sells it — not the glamour, but the wide, patient land.

  1. Book studio tours ahead. Availability and access change and often sell out.
  2. Budget for LA driving. The locations are scattered, so plan a route and allow time between stops.
  3. Check public access. Many spots are public landmarks, but some are private sets — respect boundaries.
  4. Bring a camera. Between the observatory views and the desert rocks, the photo ops are unbeatable.
Getting around: A rental car is the practical way to cover Los Angeles and reach the desert and mountain locations beyond it. Plan jumps of several hours between far-flung spots and leave early.
