Pacific Coast Highway Guide: Highway 1, Big Sur & Best Stops

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide

The Pacific Coast Highway — mostly signed as Highway 1 — is the road trip of a lifetime. It threads between coastal bluffs and the Pacific Ocean for hundreds of miles, past surf beaches, sea cliffs, small towns and some of the most photographed vistas in North America. This guide covers how to drive it, the stops worth making, and the timing that makes it work.

Route Overview

Most first-timers drive the Big Sur / central coast portion, usually between Santa Monica in the south and San Francisco in the north. The full distance is roughly 450 miles. Plan four to six days for a relaxed pace with time to stop; two days is doable but rushed.

Must-See Stops Along Highway 1

Malibu & the Santa Monica Mountains

Head north from Santa Monica past a string of beaches. Point Mugu State Park offers easy coastal hiking, and the vineyard loop of Malibu adds a tasting break.

Santa Barbara

A relaxed Spanish-colonial city with a mission and a red-tile waterfront. European in feel, it’s a good first night stop.

San Luis Obispo & Morro Bay

This university town has a lively downtown. Nearby, Morro Rock and the bayside village make for a scenic afternoon.

Big Sur

The centerpiece of the drive. Set aside a full day. Key sights:

  • Bixby Creek Bridge — the famous single-span bridge, a must-photo stop.
  • Pfeiffer Big Sur State Park — redwoods and the river canyon.
  • McWay Falls (Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park) — a waterfall spilling straight onto a beach.
  • Pfeiffer Beach — purple sand and dramatic arches (reservation needed in season).

Monterey & Carmel

End the Big Sur leg in Monterey for the Aquarium and Cannery Row, or in charming Carmel-by-the-Sea. From here, Highway 1 continues past Santa Cruz toward Half Moon Bay and San Francisco.

Best Time to Drive the Pacific Coast Highway

Because much of the route hugs steep bluffs, late spring to early autumn is the safest and clearest window. Summer months (June–August) offer dry roads and long daylight, though mornings can be foggy along the coast. September’s “second summer” often brings the best clear skies. Winter and early spring is quieter but can bring rain and slides, occasionally forcing closures.

Beware of closures: Sections of Highway 1 near Big Sur occasionally close for rockslides or construction, sometimes for months. Always check Caltrans before you set out and have an inland alternative (Highway 101) in mind.

Driving Tips

  1. Fill up early. Gas stations are scarce and expensive between towns on the Big Sur stretch.
  2. Use pull-outs. Slow down and use designated overlooks — never stop on the roadway.
  3. Watch for cyclists. The route is a cycling favorite; give them plenty of room.
  4. Don’t rush. The appeal is the journey, so budget extra time and stop often.
  5. Book lodging ahead. Big Sur has limited hotels and they fill far in advance.
