California Golf Road Trip

Updated August 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

California is built for the road trip, and for the golfer it's a dream. You can start your week on the cliff-hugging fairways of Monterey, spend a day in the desert drama of Palm Springs, and finish with ocean-view golf in San Diego — all on one long, scenic drive with a different golf landscape every day. Here's how to string it together.

Route 1: The Coastal Classic

The classic starts in the north and heads south along Highway 1, mixing some of the world's best golf with America's most dramatic coastline.

  • Monterey Peninsula — the headline stop. Pebble Beach, Spyglass Hill, Spanish Bay and the budget gem Pacific Grove, all within minutes of each other.
  • Half Moon Bay (Ocean) — a beautiful cliff-top links just south of San Francisco, perfect as a warm-up.
  • Big Sur — drive it for the scenery; some of the most spectacular coastal road in the world.
  • Santa Barbara — the linksy Sandpiper offers Pacific-view golf along the coast.

Route 2: The Desert Loop

For warm-weather, wide-open desert golf, head inland to the Coachella Valley.

  • Palm Springs — the desert golf capital. Play PGA West (with the famous island-green 17th), Indian Wells, or the quieter Desert Willow.
  • Rams Hill (Borrego Springs) — a beautiful Tom Fazio desert course east of San Diego.
  • Torrey Pines (San Diego) — end the loop with the famous municipal that hosts the pro tour.

A week-long itinerary

  1. Day 1–2 — Monterey. Play Pacific Grove on arrival, then Pebble Beach the next morning. Add the 17-Mile Drive.
  2. Day 3 — Drive south along Highway 1, through Big Sur, overnight in Santa Barbara.
  3. Day 4 — Sandpiper or a Santa Barbara-area round, then continue toward Palm Springs.
  4. Day 5 — Palm Springs desert golf. PGA West or Indian Wells.
  5. Day 6 — Rams Hill or Desert Willow, then toward San Diego.
  6. Day 7 — Torrey Pines South for the grand finale.

Planning tips

A few things that make a California golf road trip go smoothly:

  • Book the icons early. Pebble Beach and Torrey Pines sell out weeks to months ahead.
  • Keep drive times realistic. The state is bigger than it looks; plan a few hours between rounds, not a marathon.
  • Balance splurge and value. Pair a big bucket-list round with affordable gems like Pacific Grove or Barona Creek.
  • Watch the season. The coast is mild year-round; the desert is best in cooler months (October–April).
Traveler's advice: A full-week tour is ambitious but unforgettable. If you have less time, pick one region — Monterey for the icons, or Palm Springs for warm desert golf — and get the most out of it. Either way, the drive is half the fun.
