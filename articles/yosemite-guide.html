Yosemite National Park Guide: Must-Sees, Valley, Seasons & Tips

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide

Yosemite National Park is a granite masterpiece of towering cliffs, thundering waterfalls, giant sequoias and wild meadows. Centered on the spectacular Yosemite Valley, it is one of the most celebrated and visited national parks in the world. This guide helps you see the essential sights and plan a great visit.

Yosemite Valley: The Main Event

Almost every first-time visitor starts in Yosemite Valley, carved by glaciers and ringed by iconic granite walls. From the valley floor you can see the park’s most famous landmarks — El Capitan, a nearly vertical 3,000-foot monolith beloved by climbers, and Half Dome, its granite dome rising above the east end. The Merced River flows through the valley, feeding the waterfalls.

Top valley sights:

  • Yosemite Falls — among the tallest waterfalls in North America.
  • Bridalveil Fall — a graceful, easily reached cascade.
  • El Capitan — viewpoint at El Capitan Meadow.
  • Half Dome & Mirror Lake — reflections and dramatic views.
  • Tunnel View — the classic overlook photographing the whole valley.

Beyond the Valley

Glacier Point

A scenic overlook above the valley offering a sweeping view of Half Dome, Vernal Fall and Nevada Fall. It’s a must for a wide panorama.

Mariposa Grove

Home to more than 500 mature giant sequoias, including the Grizzly Giant. A shuttle and easy trails make it accessible for families.

Tioga Pass & the High Country

In summer, the Tioga Road crosses the high Sierra, with alpine meadows, Tuolumne Meadows and trailheads. It’s usually snow-covered and closed November through late spring.

Seasons & Entry

Yosemite has something in every season, though each requires different planning.

SeasonConditionsNote
SpringWaterfalls at peak flowCrowded; Tioga may be closed
SummerBusiest, all roads openBook far ahead
FallQuiet, agreeable, colorsGreat time for hiking
WinterSnow, hushed & scenicLimited access, chains required
Entry reservations: Yosemite frequently operates a reservation system, especially on busy dates, and requires a vehicle entrance fee. Check the park’s official site well in advance for reservation and road-status details.

Tips for a Great Visit

  1. Arrive early. Parking fills up by mid-morning in summer; use the free valley shuttle.
  2. Stay inside the park if you can — overnight visitors get earlier access and the valley at dawn.
  3. Carry water and layers. The valley is hot in summer; the high country is much cooler.
  4. Give it time. One day covers the highlights; two to three days lets you hike and explore deeper.
  5. Respect wildlife. Bears roam the park — store food properly and keep your distance from animals.

Whatever your pace, allow a full day or two in the valley. It’s the place that defines Yosemite — and it more than lives up to the legend.
