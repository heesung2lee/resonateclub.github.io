California golf packing and baggage guide

Updated October 2026 · GoldenPath Guide
· By GoldenPath Guide Editorial Team

California golf packing fails in two predictable ways: too many warm-weather clothes for a cold coast, and a club case that the airline charges extra for. Monterey mornings sit in the 50s even in July while Palm Springs afternoons top 100 in the same month. Pack for two climates inside one suitcase and confirm your airline golf policy before leaving home.

This guide covers club transport, airline baggage rules, clothing by region, and the small items golfers always forget. Print the checklist at the end and pack against it the night before.

Getting clubs there: bring or rent

A hard or padded golf travel case with a stiff arm protector remains the safest way to fly with clubs. Remove the driver head or fit the protector rod, wrap headcovers around iron heads for padding, and fill dead space with towels and rain gear. Photograph the packed case before closing it, because damage claims need proof of condition.

Most major airlines count a golf bag as one piece of checked baggage within normal size and weight limits, commonly 50 pounds, with oversize fees above roughly 62 linear inches. Policies change by carrier and fare class, so check your airline page within two weeks of flying. Club rental at California resorts runs to a full premium set from major brands, and the quality genuinely suits single-digit players at places like Pebble Beach and PGA West.

  • Flying with your own clubs wins for trips of 3 or more rounds or golfers with fitted equipment.
  • Renting wins for 1 to 2 rounds, tight connections, or multi-city trips with small rental cars.
  • Ship clubs door to door only for trips longer than 10 days, since transit takes 3 to 5 business days each way.
  • Always carry gloves, balls for one round, tees, and a rangefinder in hand baggage as delay insurance.

Clothing by region and season

Monterey and San Francisco demand layers year-round. A morning round starts near 55 degrees with marine fog, warms to the mid-60s by noon, and cools again after 3 p.m. Pack a windproof mid-layer, a light rain shell, and a warm hat even in August. Shorts work at midday but long trousers get more use than visitors expect.

San Diego is the easy case: mild days from the mid-60s to mid-70s most of the year, cool evenings near the water, and sun that burns through haze. Palm Springs splits hard by season, with winter highs in the 65 to 75 range and summer highs above 100. Desert winter mornings start near 45 degrees, so the first three holes need a jacket that comes off by the 5th. Our season and packing guides break each month down further.

Packing tip: Lay out one full rain outfit, one wind outfit, and one heat outfit regardless of forecast. California microclimates deliver all three inside a single week, and pro shops charge resort prices for emergency gear.

Shoes, gloves, and consumables

Bring broken-in shoes with soft spikes or spikeless soles, since metal spikes are banned nearly everywhere. A second pair matters on trips with back-to-back walking rounds, because wet or compressed shoes cost comfort by day three. Two gloves minimum, three for summer desert trips where sweat kills grip by the back nine.

Balls, tees, and sunscreen vanish faster than planned. Budget at least a sleeve per round for coastal courses with water and canyons, more at Pete Dye designs. Sunscreen needs reapplying every 2 hours in desert sun, and lip balm with SPF earns its pocket space. Rangefinders and GPS watches are legal at nearly all resort play; confirm the local rule on slope functions before a competitive round. A portable phone charger earns its weight on golf days, since GPS apps drain batteries fast in areas with weak signals like canyon courses. A small first-aid pouch with bandages, pain relief, and any personal medication rides in the bag every round without adding real weight.

Carry-on golf kit for delay day

Delayed golf bags destroy arrival-day rounds more often than any other disruption. A carry-on kit saves the day: glove, balls for one round, tees, ball markers, sunscreen stick, blister tape, and any prescription items. Wear or carry golf shoes if space allows, since rental shoes fit poorly and blister fast.

Confirm the rental car fits the group bags before signing. Four golfers with travel cases need a full-size SUV or minivan, not a midsize sedan, and California trunk space ratings run optimistic. Photograph the loaded trunk at pickup so the return-day repack takes minutes.

Printable packing checklist

  1. Travel case, stiff-arm rod, rain cover, and luggage tag with phone number.
  2. Driver, woods, irons, wedges, putter, and alignment sticks if you use them.
  3. Two pairs of golf shoes, three gloves, and blister tape.
  4. Wind layer, rain shell, warm mid-layer, and one heat outfit.
  5. Collared shirts for each round plus one spare, trousers or shorts per course dress code.
  6. Balls by the sleeve-per-round rule, tees, markers, divot tool, and towel.
  7. Rangefinder, charger, sunscreen, lip balm, hat, and sunglasses.
  8. Small bills for tipping, booking confirmations, and a copy of the airline baggage policy.

Pack against the list, weigh the case, and leave 3 pounds of margin under the limit. The souvenirs will fill it on the way home.
