Solvang Travel Guide: Danish Village History, Wine & Family Day

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

You round a bend in the Santa Ynez Valley and the roofline shifts from California ranch to Lego diorama in about a hundred yards — half-timbered walls, a working windmill, and a little bronze mermaid who looks very far from Copenhagen. Solvang opened in 1911 as a Danish colony and never looked back. It is also, happily, planted in one of the state’s better wine regions, which is why this easy day trip from Santa Barbara tends to stretch into an evening.

A Short History of Solvang

In 1911 a group of Danish settlers picked this spot for its sunny California climate and founded a colony they named after the Danish words for “sunny field.” Over the decades the town leaned hard into its heritage, dressing its main street in classic Danish architecture until the place became one of the most distinctive small towns in the state — a little slice of Scandinavia on the Central Coast.

What to See & Do

The Danish-Style Downtown

Nearly everything worth seeing sits on a compact, walkable downtown. Wander the streets to admire the half-timbered storefronts, the working windmill and a replica of Copenhagen’s Little Mermaid statue. The vibe is festive and almost aggressively photogenic.

Bakeries & Sweets

Solvang is best known for its Danish pastries, and aebleskiver — little spherical pancakes that arrive dusted with powdered sugar — plus freshly baked kringle are the standards. Stop at one of the traditional bakeries and sit on a patio with a coffee.

  • Danish pastries & aebleskiver — the classic treat.
  • Chocolate and candy shops — good stock for souvenirs.
  • The Solvang Windmill — a local landmark.

Museums & Attractions

The Elverhøj Museum of History & Art traces the town’s Danish roots, while the Hans Christian Andersen Museum honors the storyteller who gave the world so many of its fairy tales. Traveling with kids, they will love the OstrichLand and Ostrich Farm just outside town, where you can feed ostriches and emus.

Wine & the Santa Ynez Valley

Solvang sits in the heart of a celebrated wine region. The Santa Ynez Valley is known for cool-climate Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, and there are dozens of tasting rooms within an easy drive. A tasting afternoon pairs nicely with a wander through the village.

  1. Downtown tasting rooms — several wineries have rooms right in town.
  2. Nearby wineries — drive out to the valley’s vineyards.
  3. Pair with Los Olivos — the neighboring village, also full of tasting rooms and boutiques.
Best times to visit: Year-round weather is mild. Spring and fall are ideal for comfortable days and wine tasting. The town hosts festive events in winter (a traditional Danish Christmas market) and spring — check the calendar for food and wine festivals.

Making It a Family Day

Solvang is compact and easy to stroll, which makes it a relaxed stop for kids. Combine the wineries (for the adults) with the ostrich farm, a pastry stop and a ride on one of the small horse-drawn or pedal vehicles around town. With its storybook look, it is a gentle, memorable break on a Central Coast drive.

Getting There

Solvang is about a 45-minute drive north of Santa Barbara along a scenic route. It works as an easy day trip from the coast, or a restful overnight stop on a San Francisco to Los Angeles coastal journey. Parking is on the streets or in the small lots downtown.
