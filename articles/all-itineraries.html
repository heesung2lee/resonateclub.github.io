California Itineraries: Ready-Made Trip Routes & Day Plans

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide

Planning a California trip can feel overwhelming given how much there is to see. These ready-made itineraries remove the guesswork — each spells out day-by-day routes, where to overnight and what to prioritize. Pick the one that matches your trip length and style, then adapt it to your interests.

Which Itinerary Fits You?

  • 5-day Southern California — Los Angeles to San Diego via the coast. Great for first-timers, families and a relaxed pace with beaches and iconic sights.
  • 7-day Northern California — San Francisco, Napa and the Pacific Coast Highway down through Big Sur. Perfect for scenery, wine and that famous coastal drive.

How the Routes Work

Both itineraries are designed to minimize backtracking and keep driving to a comfortable amount each day. You’ll typically base yourself in a coastal city, spend the day exploring, and overnight within a reasonable distance of the next morning’s stop.

  1. Keep travel light. Pack for flexibility — you’ll be moving hotels most days.
  2. Book lodging in advance. Popular spots (Santa Barbara, Monterey, Dead-Line tourist towns) sell out in peak season.
  3. Allow buffer time. Traffic in Los Angeles and on Highway 1 can add an hour; don’t over-schedule.

Can I Combine Them?

Yes. If you have two weeks, you can link the two routes into one grand north-to-south coastal journey — driving the Pacific Coast Highway from the Bay Area down through Big Sur, Santa Barbara and on to San Diego. That makes a classic, unforgettable California coast trip.

Fresh Routes Every Season

Season matters: The itineraries work year-round, but check timing. Winter brings cooler, sometimes rainy coastal weather and occasional Highway 1 closures; summer brings warmth and crowds; spring and fall offer the best balance. See the travel-tips guides for the best time to visit and what to pack.

Whichever route you choose, remember the golden rule of California travel: don’t crowd the days. The state rewards a slower pace and a willingness to stop for the view.
