Rental Car California Guide: Insurance, Tolls & Driving Tips

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

Thirty miles between two wineries, and the only shuttle is the one that keeps the workers off the road. That is the reality of California: outside the big cities, a car isn’t a convenience, it’s the whole game. The trick is renting one without getting hit by fees you didn’t see coming.

Here’s what matters at the counter, on the toll road, and in the mountains.

Why You Need a Car

San Francisco, Los Angeles and San Diego are built around freeways, and the stuff people drive out here for — the coast, the mountains, the wine country, the national parks — sits far beyond any transit line. A rental car means you set the schedule. Just budget for the traffic that comes with it.

Insurance: What You Need

Insurance is where rental companies make their money, so go in armed. The agent will run through a list of coverages at the counter; don’t accept them until you’ve checked what your own policy and credit card already cover.

  • Collision Damage Waiver (CDW) — limits your liability for damage to the rental car.
  • Liability insurance — covers damage you cause to others (you may already have this through a personal policy or card).
  • Your credit card — many cards include secondary rental coverage; call your issuer to confirm.
Check your cards & policy first: Many premium credit cards include rental car collision coverage when you pay for the rental with that card. Decline the company’s CDW if you’re already covered — just make sure you have liability coverage too.

Driving Rules & Requirements

  1. Minimum age — most companies rent to drivers 21+, but charge a “young driver” fee under 25.
  2. License — a valid driver’s license is required; an international permit is helpful for non-English licenses.
  3. Right turn on red — allowed after a complete stop, unless a sign prohibits it.
  4. Child seats — required for young children; request one when booking.
  5. Gas — return it full or accept the company’s refueling charge (usually more expensive).

    6. Tolls & the Fastrak

    California runs several toll bridges and express-lane systems, and most rental cars ship with a FasTrak transponder already in the mirror. You can also go with the License Plate / Toll-by-Plate option. Driving through with the transponder is the easy route and covers express lanes, but you’ll pay the toll plus a rental-company processing fee. If you’d rather dodge all of it, stick to the free roads and bridges.

    Parking & Driving Tips

    • Downtown parking is pricey. Look for garages, or skip it entirely and use a rideshare in places like San Francisco and downtown San Diego.
    • Pay attention to meters. Street parking is usually time-limited and actively monitored.
    • Read the parking signs. Ramp hours, street cleaning and residential permit zones all apply, and the sticker penalties are steep.
    • Plan for traffic. Los Angeles freeways are busy at most hours; leave buffer time.

    Chains & Winter Driving

    In the mountains — Tahoe, Yosemite, the Sierra — drivers must carry tire chains in winter conditions, and some roads require chains to be installed on the spot. If you’ll be driving at elevation in snow season, ask the rental company about chains before you head up; they’re not always in the car.

    Sort out the insurance, the tolls and the chains, and the rest is easy. A rental car turns California from a list of places into a trip you steer yourself.
