Rental Car California Guide: Insurance, Tolls & Driving Tips

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

A rental car is the easiest way to explore California, where attractions are spread far apart and public transit covers only the big cities. But renting involves some specific considerations — insurance, tolls, age rules and parking. This guide covers everything you need to drive California with confidence.

Why You Need a Car

California’s urban areas (San Francisco, Los Angeles, San Diego) are large and car-dependent, and the statewide attractions — the coast, mountains, wine country and national parks — are nearly impossible to reach without driving. A rental car gives you freedom to go at your own pace.

Insurance: What You Need

Understanding rental car insurance saves you money and avoids surprises. The rental company will offer several coverages. Before agreeing, check what your own policy or credit card already covers.

  • Collision Damage Waiver (CDW) — limits your liability for damage to the rental car.
  • Liability insurance — covers damage you cause to others (you may already have this through a personal policy or card).
  • Your credit card — many cards include secondary rental coverage; call your issuer to confirm.
Check your cards & policy first: Many premium credit cards include rental car collision coverage when you pay for the rental with that card. Decline the company’s CDW if you’re already covered — just make sure you have liability coverage too.

Driving Rules & Requirements

  1. Minimum age — most companies rent to drivers 21+, but charge a “young driver” fee under 25.
  2. License — a valid driver’s license is required; an international permit is helpful for non-English licenses.
  3. Right turn on red — allowed after a complete stop, unless a sign prohibits it.
  4. Child seats — required for young children; request one when booking.
  5. Gas — return it full or accept the company’s refueling charge (usually more expensive).

    6. Tolls & the Fastrak

    California has several toll bridges and express lane systems. Many rental cars come with a FasTrak transponder, or you can use the License Plate / Toll-by-Plate option. Paying by transponder is convenient and covers express lanes, but you’ll be billed for tolls plus a rental-company processing fee. If you prefer to avoid tolls, use free routes and bridges where possible.

    Parking & Driving Tips

    • Downtown parking is pricey. Look for garages or use rideshare in dense city cores (San Francisco, downtown San Diego).
    • Pay attention to meters. Street parking is often time-limited and monitored.
    • Check parking signs — ramp/street sanitations and residential permits apply in many neighborhoods.
    • Be ready for traffic — Los Angeles freeways are busy; allow extra time.

    Chains & Winter Driving

    In the mountains (Tahoe, Yosemite, the Sierra), drivers must carry tire chains in winter conditions, and some roads require chains to be installed. If your trip involves mountain driving in snow season, ask your rental company about chains — they’re not always included.

    With a little preparation, renting a car in California is straightforward and opens up the whole state. Happy driving.
