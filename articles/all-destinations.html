California Destinations: A Complete Travel Guide

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

California packs an astonishing range of landscapes into a single state — rugged Pacific cliffs, world-class beaches, high alpine lakes, granite cathedrals, old-growth forests and a desert that blooms in spring. For travelers, that variety is both a gift and a planning challenge. This guide is the starting point for the state’s most rewarding destinations, each covered in more depth on its own page.

Whether you have a long weekend or a three-week road trip, the places below deliver the scenery, food and culture that make California worth crossing a continent for.

Why California Is a Traveler’s Paradise

California spans roughly 840 miles from the Oregon border to Mexico, with a coastline of nearly 800 miles. Within that space you find some of the nation’s oldest trees, its tallest peaks, and one of the highest concentrations of scenic byways anywhere. The state’s mild climate means there is no single “off” season — you simply adjust your plan to the region.

  • Great variety — beach, mountain, desert and forest all reachable within a long weekend.
  • Famous landmarks — Golden Gate Bridge, Yosemite Valley, Big Sur cliffs, Lake Tahoe.
  • Food and wine — coastal seafood, Napa and Sonoma wines, farm-to-table dining.
  • Easy road trips — well-maintained highways link most major attractions.

The Essential California Destinations

Pacific Coast Highway (Highway 1)

The most iconic drive in the country, hugging the coastline from Santa Monica north through Big Sur and toward San Francisco. Expect dramatic headlands, coastal towns and pull-offs every few miles.

San Diego

Southern California’s laid-back seaside city, home to La Jolla’s coves, the sprawling Balboa Park, miles of beaches and the historic Gaslamp Quarter.

Lake Tahoe

A deep alpine lake straddling the California–Nevada border, ringed by ski resorts and crystal-clear beaches. Spectacular in every season.

Yosemite National Park

One of the world’s most celebrated national parks, famous for El Capitan, Half Dome, giant sequoias and thundering waterfalls.

Redwood National & State Parks

Home to the tallest trees on Earth, this cluster of parks in far Northern California offers quiet groves and scenic drives.

Solvang

A Danish-themed village in the Santa Ynez Valley, known for windmills, bakeries and nearby wineries.

Pro tip: California is big and traffic can be slow. Pick a region or two per trip rather than trying to see everything — you’ll enjoy it far more.

How to Use This Guide

Each destination link below has its own detailed article with recommended stops, the best time to visit and seasonal tips. For routes and day-by-day planning, see the Itineraries section; for practical planning like when to go, renting a car and what to pack, head to Travel Tips.

  1. Reduce your shortlist. Choose a region that matches your trip length.
  2. Read the destination guide. Note the highlights and seasonal notes.
  3. Build a route. Use an itinerary to tie it together.
  4. Plan logistics. Book a rental car and pack for the season.
Know before you go: Several attractions require advanced reservations. Yosemite often uses a timed entry system, and some coastal state parks require parking permits. Check official park sites before you visit.
