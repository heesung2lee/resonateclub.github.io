California Destinations: A Complete Travel Guide

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

Drive north out of San Francisco and the fog rolls in thick over the hills, the Pacific hammering shale far below. Turn east and you are in granite country, where El Capitan's face catches the last light. Twelve hours south, palm trees lean over a boardwalk in sand still warm from the sun. That is the trick of California: it is not one place. It is a dozen, stacked inside a single state's borders, and the distance between them is small enough to feel like a cheat.

The range is the whole point. About 840 miles separates the Oregon line from Mexico, and the coastline alone runs nearly 800. Here you find the oldest trees alive and some of the tallest peaks in the lower forty-eight, deserts that bloom overnight after rain, and more scenic byways per mile than almost anywhere in the country. Because the climate is mild coast to coast, there is no true closing season — you just shift your plan to the region in front of you.

Why California Rewards the Trip

Few places let you swim in the morning and stand in a snowfield by evening. That compression is what makes the state so easy to love and so hard to plan. A handful of things it does better than anywhere else:

  • Scale and variety — beach, mountain, desert and forest, all reachable inside a long weekend.
  • Landmarks that live up to it — the Golden Gate in a fogbank, Yosemite Valley at sunrise, Big Sur's cliffs, Tahoe's blue.
  • Food and wine — Dungeness crab off the dock, Napa cabernet, tomatoes that taste like the coast.
  • Roads built for the trip — well-kept highways string the biggest draws together.

The Essential California Destinations

Pacific Coast Highway (Highway 1)

The most famous drive in the country, and for good reason. It hugs the edge of the continent from Santa Monica north through Big Sur toward San Francisco — headlands dropping into surf, fishing towns, and a pull-off around almost every turn.

San Diego

Southern California at its most unhurried. La Jolla's coves shelter sea lions, Balboa Park sprawls across a thousand acres, and the Gaslamp Quarter hums after dark. Come for the beaches, stay for the low-key rhythm.

Lake Tahoe

A cold, impossibly clear alpine lake on the California–Nevada border, ringed by ski resorts and sand that gleams in summer. It turns from snowfield to swim spot and back again, and it is worth seeing in both guises.

Yosemite National Park

Few places photograph better than they look. Yosemite is one of them. El Capitan, Half Dome, the giant sequoias, and waterfalls that fill the valley with a distant, constant roar.

Redwood National & State Parks

The tallest trees on Earth grow in this far-northern cluster, where the fog hangs in the canopy and the silence is thick enough to feel. Quiet groves and slow scenic drives reward the long haul north.

Solvang

A Danish village dropped into the Santa Ynez Valley — windmills, bakeries, and wine country a short drive away. Quirky, and better than it has any right to be.

Santa Barbara

Spanish colonial arches, a promenade of palms and a relaxed harbor — often called the American Riviera, with wine country sitting quietly inland.

Santa Monica

LA's oceanfront anchor: the pier, the beach and the palisades path. A day here is equal parts city and coast.

San Luis Obispo

A bright, sun-warmed college town on the Central Coast — a relaxed stop between the Sierras and Big Sur.

Pasadena

Old town shops, the arroyo and the Rose Bowl, backed by the San Gabriel Mountains. A gentler side of LA.

Sacramento & the State Capitol

California's capital — historic gold-rush architecture, a lively waterfront and the seat of state government.

Sequoia & Kings Canyon

Home to General Sherman and the largest trees on Earth by volume, set among granite peaks and deep canyon.

Mammoth Lakes

A year-round alpine base in the Eastern Sierra — skiing in winter, lakes and trails in summer.

Mendocino County

The North Coast's dreamy seaside towns, bluffs and farm-to-table food. California at its most unhurried.

TCL Chinese Theatre

Hollywood's landmark movie palace and its famous handprints-in-cement forecourt.

SFMOMA

San Francisco's modern art treasure — six floors of painting, photography and sculpture.

Comic & Sci-Fi Tour

A geek's guide to California — the film locations, studios and museums behind beloved movies and shows.

Gray Whale Migration

Each winter, gray whales pass close to the coast — a moving annual spectacle best seen from the shore.

Pro tip: California is big and traffic is slow. Pick one or two regions per trip instead of trying to see everything — you will enjoy it far more.

How to Use This Guide

Each destination page below goes deeper — recommended stops, the best season, and the small things that make it worth your time. For routes and day-by-day plans, see the Itineraries section; for rental cars, packing and timing, head to Travel Tips.

  1. Trim your shortlist. Choose a region that matches the days you actually have.
  2. Read the guide. Note the highlights and the seasonal caveats.
  3. Build a route. Use an itinerary to tie the stops together.
  4. Sort the logistics. Book the rental car and pack for the season.
Know before you go: Several attractions require advance reservations. Yosemite often runs a timed-entry system, and some coastal state parks ask for parking permits. Check official park sites before you visit.
