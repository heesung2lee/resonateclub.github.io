California Skiing: Lake Tahoe, Mammoth & the Sierra Slopes

Updated AUGUST 2026 · GoldenPath Guide
· By GoldenPath Guide Editorial Team

Californians ski the way they do most things — punctuated by sunshine. A few hours’ drive from the state’s major cities, the Sierra Nevada rises into an alpine playground of granite peaks, deep snowpacks and resorts that rival the Rockies. Whether you are chasing fresh tracks in Tahoe or cruising the expansive slopes of the Eastern Sierra, California skiing is a road-trip adventure that rewards a little planning. Stepping out of the car, the first cold snap of the air lands like a surprise after all that warm coast.

Lake Tahoe: The Big Three

The Lake Tahoe area is the state’s ski mecca, ringed by resorts on every shore. Three stand out for their scale and reputation. Palisades Tahoe is the largest, with steep terrain, a celebrated Olympic pedigree and sweeping views of the lake below. Heavenly sits on the Nevada side and is famous for its high-altitude runs and the gondola that lifts skiers straight above the water, the lake flashing deep blue through the spokes of the lift as you rise. Nearby, Northstar and Kirkwood add family-friendly cruisers and serious off-piste terrain, so a single Tahoe trip can cover a wide range of styles. The clear-blue lake and forested shores make the scenery every bit as memorable as the skiing.

Mammoth Mountain: The Eastern Sierra Giant

On the far side of the range, Mammoth Mountain in the Eastern Sierra is legendary for its massive annual snowfall and vast, sunlit bowls. Its celebrated terrain park is a draw for freestyle riders, while the long, open runs suit everyone from beginners to experts. Mammoth is also known for a remarkably long season — it regularly stays open into June, and on big years the snow lingers well into summer. The town of Mammoth Lakes below offers lodging and lively après.

Mt Shasta & Big Bear

Two very different picks round out the map. Far to the north, Mt Shasta rises out of the valley as a striking volcano, and its compact resort offers an unpretentious, lower-key experience with the mountain looming overhead. Down south, Big Bear serves Los Angeles and San Diego as the closest big mountain, an easy weekend getaway with two resorts, a friendly village and reliable snow that keeps Southern California skiing within reach of the beach.

Season, Powder & Après

Skiers choose between the big Tahoe hubs, which pack three or four resorts into one trip, and the quieter, longer-running Mammoth by itself. Whatever you pick, the views are the constant: powder-covered pines, high alpine bowls and, on a clear day, the deep blue lake or the desert floor stretching out below. For first-timers it is worth taking a lesson, as the Sierra’s dry, light snow makes for an appealing day on gentle runs before you progress.

The core season runs from November through April, with the most reliable snow typically arriving from December to March. Powder days follow big Sierra storms — check the forecast and be ready to go midweek for the freshest lines before the crowds. Après ski culture is strong at Tahoe and Mammoth, where lodges warm up with firesides and drinks after the lifts close.

  • Driving time — Tahoe is about 3–4 hours from San Francisco or an hour from Reno; Mammoth is roughly 5 hours from Los Angeles, 3 from Vegas.
  • Gear rental — most resorts rent skis, boards, boots and helmets on site, so you can travel light.
  • Lift tickets — buy ahead to avoid peak-day pricing and save money.
Travel tip: Sierra weather changes fast. Pack layers, carry chains (checking the law on mountain passes), and allow extra time for the route — snowstorms can slow the drive from the city. But the first time the slope drops away in front of you — everyone gone quiet, the snow sparkling — you'll agree the detour was worth it.
