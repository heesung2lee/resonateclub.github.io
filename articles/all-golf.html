California Golf

California Golf Guide

Updated August 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

Few states can match California for the sheer variety of golf. In a single trip you can play an ocean-side links in fog on the coast, a municipal championship high above the Pacific in San Diego, and a desert layout cut through rock and boulder in Palm Springs — often within a few hours’ drive of one another. This richness, combined with a climate that allows year-round play, is why the state draws golfers from around the world.

This section gathers our guides to the state’s most worthwhile public and resort courses, plus practical advice on when and where to play. Everything here is independent, informational content — no booking services, no sales push, just honest pointers to help you plan your own rounds and decide where your money and time are best spent.

Start here

If you only read one page, make it the best-value public courses guide. California has some of the highest greens fees in the country, but it also has some of the best municipal and daily-fee courses anywhere. Knowing which of the state’s affordable layouts punch above their price is the single most useful thing for a visiting golfer, and it can transform a trip from a budget drain into a genuine highlight.

The three golfing regions

Understanding the state’s geography makes planning far easier, because the character of a California golf round changes dramatically with the terrain.

The coast

From San Diego up through Monterey and the Bay Area, coastal courses enjoy a mild, consistent climate. Expect ocean views, coastal breeze and often a persistent marine layer that keeps things cool and damp. Torrey Pines and Pebble Beach both live here.

The inland valley

The Central Valley, the Inland Empire and parts of the LA metro sit inland where summer temperatures climb well past the 90s. These courses are best played in spring and fall, and they tend to be more affordable and less crowded than the famous coastal names.

The desert

The Coachella Valley and other desert areas are a winter-golf paradise. Warm afternoons, dramatic mountain backdrops and generous fairways characterize courses like PGA West. But summer is genuinely extreme — timing the season is everything.

How we recommend you build a golf trip

  1. Pick your region first. Coastal courses play cool and often breezy; desert courses play warm and dry; inland courses sit somewhere between. Align the region with the season and your own preferences for heat versus chill.
  2. Check the season. Desert golf is a winter sport, coastal golf (especially the north coast) is an all-day summertime affair, and the best months inland are spring and fall.
  3. Book tee times well ahead. The most famous courses — Torrey Pines South, Pebble Beach, PGA West — sell out days to weeks in advance. The longer your notice, the better the times you’ll secure.
  4. Pair golf with the local scene. A round in Temecula pairs naturally with a wine-country afternoon; Palm Springs golf can be tied in with Coachella Valley restaurants and hiking.
Good to know: Many of California’s best courses are municipally owned and open to the public by reservation — no membership required. Read each course’s policy on peak-season surcharges and cart requirements before you book, as these vary widely and can significantly affect the cost of a round.

A few things to keep in mind

Because the state’s championship courses are long and exposed, choosing the right tee box is essential to enjoying the day. Dress in layers for the coast, and remember that sunscreen and a hat are essential almost everywhere — the California sun is strong even in the cooler months. Walking is popular and encouraged on many coastal layouts.

Wherever you play, the state rewards those who plan ahead, respect the weather and pick courses suited to their game. With the guides in this section, you’ll be well prepared to enjoy everything California golf has to offer.
