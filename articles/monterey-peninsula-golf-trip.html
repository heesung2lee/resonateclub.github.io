Monterey Peninsula Golf Trip

Updated August 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

There is no concentration of great golf like it anywhere. On a single stretch of California coastline, the Monterey Peninsula packs three bucket-list resort courses, a handful of charming public tracks and enough scenery to make you forget your scorecard. It's the closest thing to a golf paradise that you can actually play — more accessible than Cypress Point's private gates, and almost all of it within a short drive of Monterey and Carmel. The ocean air moves through the fairways cool and brine-soaked, and the cypress lean every which way from decades of wind.

The three resorts

The Pebble Beach Resort trio is the headline act, and each has its own character.

  • Pebble Beach Golf Links — the icon. Cliff-hugging, historic and famous worldwide. Budget for it, book way ahead, and expect a round you'll never forget.
  • Spyglass Hill — the hardest of the three, long and tree-lined through the cypress and dunes. Purists often rate it the better test.
  • The Links at Spanish Bay — the most affordable of the trilogy, a windy links-style round with a famous sunset bagpiper. If you can only play one "new" resort course here, this is a delightful pick.

The budget gem

Not every great Monterey round costs a fortune. Pacific Grove Golf Links — affectionately called the "poor man's Pebble Beach" — is a public course right on the bay. It's charming, affordable and punches far above its price, making it the perfect complement to a splurge round or a casual addition to your trip.

A two- or three-day itinerary

Two days is enough for a great golf weekend; three lets you slow down and enjoy the coast.

  1. Day 1 — Arrive, play Pacific Grove or Spanish Bay, then drive the famous 17-Mile Drive for the scenery.
  2. Day 2 — The big one: Pebble Beach. Book morning, then relax in Carmel afterward.
  3. Day 3 (optional) — The challenge: Spyglass Hill, or a leisurely round at Spanish Bay before heading home.

Beyond the golf

The Peninsula rewards time away from the course too. Carmel-by-the-Sea is a storybook village of galleries and cottages, Monterey's Cannery Row offers seafood and history, and the coastline at Big Sur is a short, spectacular drive south along Highway 1. Stand at the water’s edge and the spray cools your face while the cypress smell hangs in the air.

Traveler's advice: Book the resort courses well in advance — Pebble Beach especially sells out. Pair one big splurge round with the affordable Pacific Grove or Spanish Bay to balance cost, and add the 17-Mile Drive for the scenery that made this stretch famous.

Getting there

Monterey is about a two-hour drive south from San Francisco along the coast, or a similar distance north from Big Sur. The Monterey Regional Airport plus nearby San Jose and San Francisco make it easy to reach by air too. A car is essential for getting between the courses and towns. By the time the fog rolls back off the bay, the reason people plan whole trips around a single tee time becomes obvious.
