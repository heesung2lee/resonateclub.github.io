California Golf Season & Weather

Updated August 2026 · GoldenPath Guide
· By GoldenPath Guide Editorial Team

California is one of the few places where you can play golf in every month of the year. But “golf season” here is regional — the best time to play the desert is the exact opposite of the best time to play the coast or the mountains. This guide breaks down the state’s golfing calendar by region so you can time your trip to match what you want out of the round.

There’s a particular California feeling that only golfers know — the fog still shredding off the fairway as you tee off, the air cool and damp one moment and golden the next, the whole round laid out ahead of you like a gift.

The three broad climate zones

Coastal

From San Diego up through Monterey and the San Francisco Bay Area, the coast is mild and remarkably consistent, usually between the high 50s and the 70s. A persistent marine layer can bring morning fog and afternoon breeze, but temperatures rarely swing wildly. Summer is dependable and dry; winter brings the most cloud and the most rain, though it rarely gets truly cold.

Inland & valley

The inland valley (Sacramento, the Central Valley, parts of the Inland Empire and the LA metro interior) experiences genuine summer heat — regularly over 90°F and sometimes far hotter. These courses are best in spring and fall when days are warm but not punishing, with mornings especially pleasant. Winters can be cool but generally playable.

Desert

The Coachella Valley and other desert areas are a winter-golf paradise: crisp mornings, sunny days and comfortable temperatures from roughly October through April. Conversely, summer in the desert is extreme — many desert courses are simply impractical, and some close entirely for the hottest months. If you crave desert golf, winter and early spring are the window.

Seasonal considerations

Spring (March–May)

Widely the most comfortable statewide. The desert is cooling into its sweet spot, the coast is warming, and the inland valley is pleasant. Green courses and moderate prices make this a strong all-around choice for a golf trip. There’s a reason spring draws players out of the woodwork — the course is soft underfoot, the buds are breaking, and that first long-iron off the tee lands with a satisfying thump that says winter is finally over.

Summer (June–August)

Perfect on the coast, challenging inland, and often unbearable in the desert. Early tee times are essential in the valley and desert to beat the heat, and coastal courses are at their most reliable. This is prime time for a Monterey or Bay Area trip.

Fall (September–November)

Another excellent window. The desert cools into its prime season, the inland valley becomes lovely again, and the coast offers crisp, clear conditions. Prices can dip outside peak periods, and the weather is often ideal for a late-season getaway.

Winter (December–February)

Desert is at its best, the coast is mild but wetter, and mountain-adjacent courses can see frost delays. This is the classic time for a desert golf getaway, with the clear trade-off that the coast and valleys are cooler and rainier. Dust off the rain gear if you head to the Bay Area or Monterey.

Planning tip: In the desert and inland valleys, always book the earliest possible tee time in summer to avoid peak heat; in winter, book a mid-morning slot on the coast to let the fog burn off. Timing your round to the weather can be the difference between a great day and a grueling one.

Weather is the key variable

More than temperature, wind and marine layer shape the quality of a California round. Coastal courses play a different game in the afternoon breeze, and a heavy fog can slow greens and obscure the views. Check the marine-layer forecast for the coast and the heat index for inland and desert courses before you commit. Whatever the season, a light jacket and sunscreen cover nearly every situation.

Planning around weather & crowds

The two things that most shape a California golf trip are the weather and the crowds, and they often move together. Peak season on the coast and in the desert brings the best conditions but the highest prices and the busiest tee sheets. The shoulder months — late spring and early fall — often offer a sweet spot: good weather, more availability and friendlier rates.

Rain considerations

While California is famously dry, winter can bring heavy rain, especially to the north coast and the Central Valley. Desert courses get less rain, but summer heat is the real limiter. If you’re booking a winter trip, check the forecast and have a flexible plan — and keep an eye out for frost delays that can push back early tee times.

With a little planning around the season, you can find comfortable, enjoyable golf in California in any month of the year. And really, that’s the quiet gift of it — wherever you are and whatever the calendar says, there’s a fairway waiting with just the right weather. Some trips you remember for the score, but the ones you keep coming back to are those mornings when the fog burned off just as you hit your first drive.
