Wine & Food Guide

Updated August 2026 · GoldenPath Guide
· By GoldenPath Guide Editorial Team

California is one of the world’s great wine and food regions. From the fog-draped vineyards of the north to the sun-soaked valleys of the south, and from the food capitals of Los Angeles and San Francisco to the casual coastal scenes of San Diego, there is something delicious to discover at nearly every stop of a trip. This section collects our guides to the state’s wine regions and food scenes.

Everything in this section is practical, informational content — no booking services, no sales push, just honest pointers to help you plan your own tasting tour or food crawl, and to understand what makes each region distinctive.

Wine country

California spans a remarkable range of winemaking climates. Northern California is home to the world-famous Napa and Sonoma valleys, with their polished estates and world-class Cabernet, while the Temecula scene in Southern California is younger and more relaxed. Each rewards a different kind of trip, and the state’s wineries range from legendary estates to tiny family farms.

How to plan a wine-country visit

  1. Choose your region. Napa for polish and Cabernet, Sonoma for variety and charm, Temecula for an accessible Southern California base.
  2. Book tastings in advance. The most popular rooms — especially in Napa — fill up, particularly on weekends and in peak season.
  3. Pace it. Two to three stopovers beats a hurried dash through six.
  4. Sort out transport. Designate a driver or book a tour so the tasting is safe and relaxed.
  5. Factor in the food. Wine country pairs beautifully with farm-to-table dining and local markets.

Food scenes

California’s cooking is defined by fresh, seasonal produce and remarkable cultural diversity. Los Angeles and San Francisco are two of the country’s great eating cities, while San Diego offers a relaxed, border-influenced coastal cuisine. Exploring markets, neighborhoods and street food is the best way to understand each place, and the state’s farm-to-table movement means you’ll find exceptional ingredients everywhere.

What unites them

Underneath the differences, the state’s food culture shares a common thread: a reliance on the remarkable fresh produce, seafood and wine that California produces. Whether you’re at a taco truck, a farmers market, a seafood shack on the coast or a Michelin-starred restaurant, the quality of the ingredients shines through.

Good to know: Many California regions pair wine tasting with the state’s other strengths — golf in Temecula, coastal drives below Monterey, and farm-stand hopping on the Pacific Coast Highway. A combined leisure and culinary trip is easy to assemble.

Where to start

If you’re planning a wine-focused trip, begin with our Napa vs Sonoma comparison to pick your base, or explore Temecula if you’ll be in Southern California. For food lovers, the California food cities guide walks you through the dishes that define LA, San Francisco and San Diego. Together they cover the table and the glass — and give you a delicious itinerary to work from.

A word of advice

California rewards patience and a willingness to go off the beaten path. The best discoveries are often small, family-run rooms and neighborhood spots rather than the biggest names on the tour. Ask locals, wander into side streets, and let the state’s remarkable produce lead the way.

Whichever region you choose, the food and wine you’ll find here are a genuine highlight of any California trip — every bit as memorable as the scenery.

Combining food, wine and the wider trip

Because California packs so much into a small footprint, the food and wine are easy to pair with other sightseeing. Temecula works with a day at the beach or a round of golf, while Napa and Sonoma sit close to the coast and the Redwoods. Adding a food-focused stop to any itinerary is simple and rewarding, and it often becomes the part of a trip that travelers remember most.
