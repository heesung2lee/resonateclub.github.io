7-Day Northern California Itinerary: SF, Napa & the Big Sur Coast

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

There is a moment on Day 3, somewhere between Santa Cruz and Monterey, when the highway starts bending along the bluffs and you realize why you came. A week in Northern California runs you through a fog-cooled city, up into vineyard hills, and back down one of the great coastal roads on Earth. It is a lot, which is exactly the point.

Day 1: San Francisco

Get your bearings in the city. Ride a cable car up a steep hill, walk the Golden Gate Bridge, and drift through the painted Victorians of Alamo Square. Fisherman's Wharf is touristy but fine for the harbor; grab fresh seafood while you are there, and if you booked ahead, the ferry over to Alcatraz is worth every minute.

  • Golden Gate Bridge — walk or cycle the span, ideally in clear morning light.
  • Cable cars — a ride up Nob Hill that doubles as a full-body workout.
  • Alcatraz Island — book the night tour weeks in advance.

Day 2: Napa & Sonoma

Head north into wine country and let the day soften. Book one or two Napa Valley tastings rather than a marathon, then wander Sonoma Plaza's shaded square. Arrive early or reserve ahead — the good places fill fast.

  1. Morning — Napa Valley tastings.
  2. Afternoon — Sonoma Plaza.
  3. Evening — dinner in the wine country.
Designate a driver: Between tastings, arrange a driver or join a small tour. Drinking and driving is unsafe and breaks the law.

Day 3: Coast to Monterey

Turn south toward the Pacific. Stop in Half Moon Bay for coastal farmland and fish, and in Santa Cruz for the boardwalk and its long, unbroken beach. Land in Monterey in time for the Monterey Bay Aquarium and a slow walk along Cannery Row as the light goes.

  • Half Moon Bay — bluff-top farms and seafood shacks.
  • Santa Cruz — the historic boardwalk on the sand.
  • Monterey Bay Aquarium — worth the hype; go late in the day to dodge the crowds.

Day 4: Big Sur

This is the day. Drive the Pacific Coast Highway into Big Sur and stop at Bixby Bridge, Pfeiffer Beach, and the crescent of sand below McWay Falls. The cliffs drop into the ocean and the road twists through it all. Sleep in Big Sur if you can swing it, or drop back to Carmel — either way you'll be glad you left a full day.

  • Bixby Creek Bridge — the photo stop everyone makes; pull into the designated overlook.
  • McWay Falls (Julia Pfeiffer Burns) — a waterfall spilling straight onto the beach.
  • Pfeiffer Beach — reserve ahead in season to get through the gate.

Day 5: San Simeon & the Coast

Keep going south. Tour the grand Hearst Castle at San Simeon (timed entry, book ahead), then watch the elephant seals loaf and spar at Piedras Blancas. Overnight in San Luis Obispo, an easy town to like.

  1. Hearst Castle tour.
  2. Elephant seal rookery at Piedras Blancas.
  3. Overnight in San Luis Obispo.

Day 6: Santa Barbara

Roll into Santa Barbara, the self-styled "American Riviera." Walk the red-tile Mission Santa Barbara, do a slow afternoon on the beach, and wander the Spanish-colonial blocks. It is a city built for doing very little — let it.

Day 7: Return & Depart

Drive back to San Francisco in about five hours, or peel south toward Los Angeles if the trip goes on. If you are flying out of SFO, leave generous margin — the return drive through the Bay Area has a habit of snarling.

Tips for This Route

Book ahead for Alcatraz, Hearst Castle and any Big Sur lodging — all three sell out fast. Winter can demand snow chains on the mountain passes, and a stretch of Highway 1 occasionally closes for mudslides, so check conditions before you go. Above all, take your time: the coast is the reason for the drive, and it does not rush.
