Packing for California: Checklist by Season & Region

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

Step onto a San Francisco pier on a July morning and you’ll see half the tourists shivering in shorts while a native walks past in a wool coat. An hour later, thirty miles inland, the same shorts are fine and the coat is unbearable. That is the California packing problem in a nutshell: the state splits into microclimates that refuse to agree with each other.

The workable answer is not more stuff — it is layers. Build a small wardrobe that stacks and sheds, and you’ll adapt to whatever the afternoon serves up.

The Layering Principle

Temperature can swing twenty degrees in a couple of hours, so think in thin, combinable pieces rather than one heavy garment. A packable shell or light jacket earns its place every day, and one pair of comfortable walking shoes will cover more than you expect.

What to Pack by Season

SeasonBase ItemsExtras
SpringLight layers, jeans, teesLight rain jacket, warm layer
SummerTees, shorts, light pantsSunscreen, hat, swimwear
FallLayers, jeans, cardiganWarm jacket for evenings
WinterWarm layers, thick coatChains season; gloves, beanie

What to Pack by Region

Coastal & Big Sur

The coast stays cool and often foggy well into summer. Pack a light jacket for mornings and evenings, and use that same pair of good shoes for the cliff-top trails. Sunsets here get cold fast — the wind comes off the water.

Mountains (Tahoe, Yosemite, Sierra)

Mountain weather turns on a dime. Summer days are warm and exposed, but nights drop sharply, so bring warm layers and sturdy footwear for the trails. Winter is a different animal entirely: full cold-weather gear, gloves, a hat, and waterproof layers are non-negotiable if it rains or snows.

Desert & Inland

Desert valleys scorch in summer and cool off sharply after dark in winter. Breathable fabrics, sunscreen and a hat carry you through the day; a jacket handles the evening. The dryness means water is your friend, not an afterthought.

Cities & Wine Country

For San Francisco, Los Angeles and the wine country, casual-but-put-together works. Comfortable walking shoes and sun protection cover the daytime — and one dressier outfit is worth packing if a nice dinner is on the list.

A Minimal Packing Checklist

  • Clothing: 3–4 mix-and-match tops, 1–2 bottoms, layers, one warm jacket.
  • Footwear: Comfortable walking shoes + optional water-friendly sandals.
  • Weather: Packable rain shell, (winter) gloves & hat.
  • Essentials: Sunscreen (high SPF), sunglasses, a hat, a reusable water bottle.
  • Electronics: Charger, power bank, phone + a car charger.
  • Health: Any prescription meds, a small first-aid kit, motion-sickness pills for windy coastal roads.
Don’t overpack: California’s mild climate means you rarely need serious gear outside the winter mountains. Lead with layers and versatility — you can always add or shed a layer as the day warms or cools.

Season-Specific Extras

  1. Summer: Swimwear, beach towel and extra sunscreen — the sun is strong and reflection off the sand doubles the burn.
  2. Winter in the mountains: Tire chains (or rent a vehicle equipped to handle snow), warm boots, gloves.
  3. Spring wildflowers: A camera and comfortable shoes for the deserts and foothills.

Pack for the layers and you’ll handle the rest — the foggy mornings, the sun-warmed afternoons, the cold mountain nights that catch everyone off guard.
