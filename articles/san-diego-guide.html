San Diego Travel Guide: La Jolla, Balboa Park, Beaches & Gaslamp

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide

San Diego is Southern California’s relaxed, sun-drenched coastal city. Reputationally more about surf and family fun than nightlife, it offers a near-permanent springtime climate, a celebrated food scene and a wealth of outdoor attractions. This guide covers the neighborhoods and sights that define a great trip.

La Jolla

This cliff-top seaside community, pronounced la-HOY-a, is San Diego’s most picturesque district. The La Jolla Cove is famous for sea lions and clear water, while Torrey Pines Gliderport and the cliffs offer dramatic ocean views.

Highlights include:

  • La Jolla Cove & Children’s Pool — watch sea lions and seals bask on the rocks.
  • Torrey Pines State Reserve — coastal trails above the ocean.
  • Birch Aquarium — hands-on marine exhibits overlooking the bay.
  • Whale watching — gray whales pass offshore in winter.

Balboa Park

Balboa Park is a 1,200-acre urban park housing more than a dozen museums, beautiful gardens and the world-famous San Diego Zoo. It grew out of expositions and is filled with Spanish colonial architecture. You can easily spend a full day here — pick a few museums or dedicate a day to the zoo.

Popular picks include the zoo, the Natural History Museum, the Museum of Us and the Japanese Friendship Garden. The park is walkable, with free shuttle options.

Beaches & Coastal Neighborhoods

San Diego’s beaches each have their own character:

  • Mission Beach & Pacific Beach — boardwalk vibes, surfing and lively eateries.
  • Coronado — a family-friendly island beach by the historic Hotel del Coronado.
  • La Jolla Shores — calm water, ideal for families and kayaking.
  • Ocean Beach — alternative, laid-back and dog-friendly.

Gaslamp Quarter & Downtown

The historic Gaslamp Quarter is San Diego’s nightlife and dining core, set among Victorian buildings. It’s within walking distance of Petco Park (home of the Padres), the waterfront and Seaport Village. Come here for an evening out, rooftop bars and trendy restaurants.

Distinctly San Diego

  1. Old Town San Diego State Historic Park — the city’s birthplace, with Mexican-era history.
  2. Cabrillo National Monument — panoramic views from Point Loma.
  3. Little Italy — excellent Italian dining in a gentrified waterfront quarter.
  4. San Diego Bay cruises — harbor views of the naval base and skyline.

Best Time to Visit

San Diego is mild year-round. Spring (March–May) and early autumn (September–October) are ideal, with warm, agreeable weather and fewer crowds. Summer brings peak crowds and sometimes gray morning marine layer (“June gloom”); winter is mild and affordable, though evenings are cool.

Getting around: There’s no downtown subway, and attractions are spread out. A rental car, rideshare or a well-planned bus/coaster route is the most practical way to cover Balboa Park, La Jolla and the beaches.
