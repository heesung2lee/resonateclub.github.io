Catalina Island Getaway

Updated August 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

There's a reason Catalina Island feels like a mini-vacation that's almost too easy to reach. An hour by ferry from the busy Los Angeles ports, it drops you into a Mediterranean-flavored harbor town with golf carts instead of cars, a domed art-deco building, the Casino (a landmark theater and ballroom) and enough laid-back beach clubs to make you forget you're still in California. This is the practical, how-to-plan-it getaway guide.

Getting there: the ferry

The Catalina Express is the classic way in, running multiple daily crossings from Long Beach, San Pedro and Dana Point. The ride takes roughly an hour and, on a clear day, is scenic enough to be part of the fun. Book your ferry in advance — summer and weekend departures sell out. Private boats and helicopter transfers are also available if you're feeling fancy.

Choosing where to stay

Avalon has lodging to suit different styles, and an overnight stay changes the whole experience once the day-trippers leave.

  • Mt Ada — perched high above Avalon Bay with sweeping views; the former Wrigley summer home.
  • Hotel Atwater — in the heart of Avalon, convenient and stylish.
  • Pavilion Hotel — steps from the beach and the harbor.
  • Banning House Lodge & Villas — a historic Craftsman-style bed & breakfast in rustic Two Harbors.

Building the perfect day

Catalina is small enough to pack a lot in, but it rewards slowing down. Here's a balanced way to fill a day.

  • Morning — Arrive, grab coffee and a pastry at the Bistro at the Atwater, then wander Avalon's waterfront.
  • Midday — Pick an adventure: the Catalina Zipline Eco Tour over Descanso Beach, or a Glass Bottom Boat ride over the marine preserve.
  • Afternoon — A Bison Expedition into the rugged interior, or a Journey to Island's End Hummer tour.
  • Evening — Dinner at Avalon Grille, cocktails at Descanso Beach Club or the new Pier 24.

One- or two-night itinerary

To really soak it in, stay overnight.

  1. Day 1 — Ferry in, check in, explore Avalon, do a zipline or glass-bottom boat, enjoy the beach club, dinner.
  2. Day 2 — Bison expedition or Wrigley Island Tour, lunch at the beach, ferry home or stay a second night for Two Harbors.

The beach clubs

Catalina's beaches are a highlight. Descanso Beach Club offers Mediterranean-style cabanas and a relaxed beachfront bar & restaurant just past the historic Casino building. At Two Harbors, the rustic Harbor Sands palapas are roomy and peaceful — perfect for a slow day in the sun away from the crowds.

Traveler's advice: Leave your car on the mainland and don't try to cram everything in. The magic of Catalina is its unhurried pace — book your ferry and the one or two tours you really want, then let the rest unfold. Golf carts, walking and the water are your transport here.

When to visit

Catalina is a year-round destination, but the best weather and calmest seas are in spring and fall. Summer is busy (and warm), while winter can be brisk but far more peaceful. If your schedule is flexible, an early-fall or spring trip balances great conditions with lighter crowds.
