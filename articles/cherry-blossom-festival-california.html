Northern California Cherry Blossom Festival

Updated AUGUST 2026 · CalTravel Guide
· By CalTravel Guide Editorial Team

When April arrives, the streets of San Francisco's Japantown flush with soft pink blossoms, and families gather beneath the boughs. Begun in 1968, the Northern California Cherry Blossom Festival is one of the oldest Japanese cultural celebrations in the United States, held over two weekends each April. It's a lively, colorful square of food, music, and community spirit.

Japantown: The Stage

The festival is staged in San Francisco's west-side Japantown. Along Post Street between Laguna and Fillmore, the entire block becomes a fairground — food stalls, traditional performances, live bands, and martial arts demonstrations. The care and dedication of the people who want to share Japanese culture is written all over the event.

Experience Japanese Culture

Beyond the sightseeing, this is a place to get hands-on. Workshops in tea ceremony (chanoyu), ikebana flower arranging, bonsai, origami, and doll-making are on offer, alongside taiko drumming and hanami (flower-viewing) traditions. The sounds of the koto, shakuhachi, and shamisen carry down the street between live traditional dance performances. And children gravitate toward a pop-culture zone where anime, J-pop, and gaming meet, including the Tekko Book Fair.

  • Food stalls — from savory street snacks to sweets, a full taste of Japan.
  • Taiko drums — booming rhythms that set the festival heartbeat.
  • Workshops — hands-on culture for all ages.

The Grand Parade

The festival's finale is its Grand Parade, starting from the Civic Center, running along Polk Street, and ending at Fillmore on Post Street. Flamboyant floats, dance troupes, and cherry-blossom paper decorations fill the route — the climax of the final day. To catch the parade from a good spot, stake out a place along the sidewalk or on a porch well ahead of time.

More Blossoms in Golden Gate Park

For the fullest floral experience, walk over to the Japanese Tea Garden in Golden Gate Park, where mature cherry trees — symbols of beauty and the fleeting nature of life in Japanese culture — make a gorgeous backdrop for photos. Together with blossoms around town, spring has truly arrived in San Francisco.

Tip: Parking in Japantown is scarce, so take transit. The Muni bus runs within a few blocks, and a rideshare makes getting there much easier.
