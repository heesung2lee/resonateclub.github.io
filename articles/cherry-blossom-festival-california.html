Northern California Cherry Blossom Festival

Updated AUGUST 2026 · GoldenPath Guide
· By GoldenPath Guide Editorial Team

When April arrives, the streets of San Francisco's Japantown flush with soft pink blossoms, and families gather beneath the boughs. Begun in 1968, the Northern California Cherry Blossom Festival is one of the oldest Japanese cultural celebrations in the United States, held over two weekends each April. It's a lively, colorful square of food, music, and community spirit — and when a breeze moves through, petals loosen from the boughs and drift down onto the shoulders of the crowd like the festival's first confetti.

Japantown: The Stage

The festival is staged in San Francisco's west-side Japantown. Along Post Street between Laguna and Fillmore, the entire block becomes a fairground — food stalls, traditional performances, live bands, and martial arts demonstrations. The care and dedication of the people who want to share Japanese culture is written all over the event.

Experience Japanese Culture

Beyond the sightseeing, this is a place to get hands-on. Workshops in tea ceremony (chanoyu), ikebana flower arranging, bonsai, origami, and doll-making are on offer, alongside taiko drumming and hanami (flower-viewing) traditions. The sounds of the koto, shakuhachi, and shamisen carry down the street between live traditional dance performances. And children gravitate toward a pop-culture zone where anime, J-pop, and gaming meet, including the Tekko Book Fair.

  • Food stalls — from savory street snacks to sweets, a full taste of Japan.
  • Taiko drums — booming rhythms that set the festival heartbeat.
  • Workshops — hands-on culture for all ages.

The Grand Parade

The festival's finale is its Grand Parade, starting from the Civic Center, running along Polk Street, and ending at Fillmore on Post Street. Flamboyant floats, dance troupes, and cherry-blossom paper decorations fill the route — the climax of the final day, and the drumbeats reach your chest before they reach your ears. To catch the parade from a good spot, stake out a place along the sidewalk or on a porch well ahead of time.

More Blossoms in Golden Gate Park

For the fullest floral experience, walk over to the Japanese Tea Garden in Golden Gate Park, where mature cherry trees — symbols of beauty and the fleeting nature of life in Japanese culture — make a gorgeous backdrop for photos. Together with blossoms around town, spring has truly arrived in San Francisco — and for a while afterward, a soft pink lingers with you, like the memory of a very short season.

Tip: Parking in Japantown is scarce, so take transit. The Muni bus runs within a few blocks, and a rideshare makes getting there much easier.

Dates, Parade Route and Free Events

The Northern California Cherry Blossom Festival runs two consecutive April weekends, with the grand parade closing the second Sunday — typically mid-to-late April, drawing over 200,000 attendees across the fortnight. The parade steps off from City Hall, rolls down Post Street through Japantown and finishes at the Peace Plaza, with the best viewing along Post between Laguna and Fillmore Streets; arrive 60 to 90 minutes early for a front-row curb spot. Street-fair booths, martial-arts demonstrations and taiko performances run 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. both weekends and cost nothing to watch. The Queen Program coronation, tea-ceremony demonstrations and select concerts carry $15 to $35 tickets. Peak blossom on the plaza's cherry trees usually lands the first festival weekend, but blooms vary by year — check the festival's social channels the week before for bloom-status updates.

Eating Through Japantown

Plan lunch inside the festival footprint, because the food stalls and Japantown kitchens are half the event. The Japan Center mall's food court serves ramen, udon and curry bowls at $12 to $18, with lines shortest before noon. Festival street stalls sell takoyaki, yakisoba, spam musubi and strawberry mochi at $8 to $14 per item — bring cash, since several vendors skip cards. Sit-down anchors on Post Street serve sushi sets at $25 to $45 and shabu-shabu dinners near $35 per person; dinner waits run 45 minutes on festival Saturdays, so book ahead or eat at 5 p.m. Benkyodo-adjacent sweet shops sell manju and mochi boxes at $10 to $20 that travel well as gifts. For a quiet break, the mall's second-floor tea rooms pour matcha sets near $12 with a view over the plaza crowds.

Where to Stay and How to Get There

Japantown sits in the Western Addition, and transit beats driving every festival weekend. The 38-Geary bus stops two blocks from the plaza, and the Japantown garage fills by 11 a.m. both Saturdays — street parking within six blocks is effectively gone by noon. BART riders exit at Civic Center and transfer to Muni for the 10-minute ride west. Hotels in Japantown itself, including the historic Japan Center property, run $220 to $350 on festival weekends; Union Square, a 15-minute bus ride east, offers more rooms at $180 to $300. Families do well at the Cathedral Hill hotels a 10-minute walk north, with free parking packages that offset the $50-plus downtown garage rates. Pair the festival with the Japanese Tea Garden in Golden Gate Park ($10 to $15 admission), a 15-minute drive west, where the cherry trees bloom on a slightly later schedule.

Blossom Viewing Beyond the Festival Weekends

The plaza trees peak around the festival, but the season stretches wider. The Japanese Tea Garden in Golden Gate Park ($10 to $15 admission) blooms slightly later, and the UC Botanical Garden in Berkeley shows cherry varieties into early May with $15 admission. Weekday mornings before 10 a.m. give the quietest hanami photos at every spot.
